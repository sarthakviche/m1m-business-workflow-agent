Motion rules.

  • One moving subject per screen. Your eye never has to choose.
  • Motion explains the product. If removing it loses nothing, it goes.
  • Your scroll stays yours. Native scrolling, with pinning through CSS sticky.
  • Transform and opacity only. That keeps animation smooth on mid-range Android phones.
  • Reduced motion is respected. Those visitors get finished frames.

The film keeps its glow.

The page is white and the film is peach. A rounded tile with a soft shadow makes that edge look intentional until the film fills the screen.

One button, always the same.

A pill with a 44 px minimum height. One filled button per screen, and hover, press and keyboard focus all respond. Try them.

Design principles we'll hold it to.

Hierarchy

Each screen has one headline, one subject and at most one filled button.

Legibility

Text meets WCAG AA at 4.5:1. The brighter orange is limited to large type, which needs 3:1.

Touch

Targets are at least 44 px. Phones get shorter pins and a tighter crop of the film, because a full 16:9 frame is too small to read there.

Access

All copy is real HTML text, the page works by keyboard, and film frames carry descriptions.

Speed

The hero is text, so it paints first. Film frames load coarse to fine. This board's 165 frames total 4.2 MB.

Control

Scroll speed is never hijacked, and backwards works exactly like forwards.

Three decisions before I build.

Tell me in chat, or just say "go" and I'll use my picks. The site will live in a landing/ folder in this repo, which already holds the film source and the brand files.

Should the film lose its captions?

The film has captions burned in, in Plus Jakarta Sans. You can see them in the study above, and they clash with SF Pro and Inter.

My pick: re-render text-free footage from the film's source and set all headlines as live text.

Where does the main button go?

The film closes on Munim.ai with no sign-up route, and I don't have a public URL for the app.

My pick: "Request access" with a short email form, unless you have a live sign-up link to use.

Is the black invoice chapter right?

It gives the page one dramatic beat and a change of rhythm. The alternative stays white from top to bottom, which is calmer and has less impact.

My pick: keep the black chapter, for this one scene only.
